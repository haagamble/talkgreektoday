## 1. Singular adjectives

An adjective agrees with its noun in gender and number. Here are four common adjectives:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Masculine</th><th scope="col">Feminine</th><th scope="col">Neuter</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>small</td><td lang="el">μικρός</td><td lang="el">μικρή</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td></tr>
<tr><td>big / large</td><td lang="el">μεγάλος</td><td lang="el">μεγάλη</td><td lang="el">μεγάλο</td></tr>
<tr><td>good</td><td lang="el">καλός</td><td lang="el">καλή</td><td lang="el">καλό</td></tr>
<tr><td>clean</td><td lang="el">καθαρός</td><td lang="el">καθαρή</td><td lang="el">καθαρό</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

In **Είμαι καλά** (“I am well”), **καλά** is an adverb meaning “well” or “fine.” It does not change with the subject. The adjective **καλός** means “good” and changes to agree with the noun: **καλός, καλή, καλό**.

## 2. Plural adjectives

For these adjectives, masculine plurals end in **-οι**, feminine plurals in **-ες**, and neuter plurals in **-α**:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th>English</th><th>Masculine</th><th>Feminine</th><th>Neuter</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>small</td><td lang="el">μικροί</td><td lang="el">μικρές</td><td lang="el">μικρά</td></tr>
<tr><td>big / large</td><td lang="el">μεγάλοι</td><td lang="el">μεγάλες</td><td lang="el">μεγάλα</td></tr>
<tr><td>good</td><td lang="el">καλοί</td><td lang="el">καλές</td><td lang="el">καλά</td></tr>
<tr><td>clean</td><td lang="el">καθαροί</td><td lang="el">καθαρές</td><td lang="el">καθαρά</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα μικρά σπίτια είναι εκεί.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The <strong>small</strong> houses are there.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Οι πόρτες είναι <strong>καθαρές</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The doors are <strong>clean</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 3. Before a noun

Put the adjective between the article and the noun: **το μικρό σπίτι** (the small house), **μια μεγάλη τσάντα** (a big bag).

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το <strong>μεγάλο</strong> βιβλίο είναι εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The <strong>big</strong> book is here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Οι <strong>μικροί</strong> σκύλοι πίνουν νερό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The <strong>small</strong> dogs are drinking water.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η μητέρα είναι κουρασμένη.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The mother is tired.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το παιδί είναι χαρούμενο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The child is happy.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Οι γονείς είναι έτοιμοι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The parents are ready.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Οι αδελφές είναι λυπημένες.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The sisters are sad.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα σπίτια είναι μεγάλα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The houses are big.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα μήλα είναι καλά.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The apples are good.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Ο πατέρας έχει ένα μικρό κινητό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The father has a small cell phone.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Βλέπω τις καθαρές καρέκλες.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I see the clean chairs.</p>
</div>

## 4. Adjectives in the accusative

Adjectives also agree with a noun in the accusative. For the adjective patterns in this lesson, the masculine form changes. The feminine and neuter forms stay the same.

<tbody>
<tr><td>masculine</td><td lang="el">μικρός</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td></tr>
<tr><td>feminine</td><td lang="el">μικρή</td><td lang="el">μικρή</td></tr>
<tr><td>neuter</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="table-scroll"><table>

<tr><td>feminine plural</td><td lang="el">μικρές</td><td lang="el">μικρές</td></tr>
<tr><td>neuter plural</td><td lang="el">μικρά</td><td lang="el">μικρά</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω <strong>μικρούς</strong> σκύλους.</p>

</div>

## 5. Adjectives from an earlier lesson

These adjectives were introduced in the lesson on **είμαι**:

- **χαρούμενος / χαρούμενη / χαρούμενο** — happy
- **κουρασμένος / κουρασμένη / κουρασμένο** — tired
- **έτοιμος / έτοιμη / έτοιμο** — ready
- **λυπημένος / λυπημένη / λυπημένο** — sad

5. I have a small bag.
6. Maria has two good books.
7. The big doors are there.
8. We have clean chairs.
9. My brother has a small dog.
10. The table is not small.
