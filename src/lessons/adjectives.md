## 1. Singular adjectives

An adjective agrees with its noun in gender and number. Here are four common adjectives:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Masculine</th><th scope="col">Feminine</th><th scope="col">Neuter</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>small</td><td lang="el">μικρός</td><td lang="el">μικρή</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td></tr>
<tr><td>big / large</td><td lang="el">μεγάλος</td><td lang="el">μεγάλη</td><td lang="el">μεγάλο</td></tr>
<tr><td>good</td><td lang="el">καλός</td><td lang="el">καλή</td><td lang="el">καλό</td></tr>
<tr><td>new</td><td lang="el">καινούριος</td><td lang="el">καινούρια</td><td lang="el">καινούριο</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

In **Είμαι καλά** (“I am well”), **καλά** is an adverb meaning “well” or “fine.” It does not change with the subject. The adjective **καλός** means “good” and changes to agree with the noun: **καλός, καλή, καλό**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Ο σκύλος είναι <strong>μικρός</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The dog is <strong>small</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η τσάντα είναι <strong>καινούρια</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The bag is <strong>new</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 2. Plural adjectives

For these adjectives, masculine plurals end in **-οι**, feminine plurals in **-ες**, and neuter plurals in **-α**:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Masculine</th><th scope="col">Feminine</th><th scope="col">Neuter</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>small</td><td lang="el">μικροί</td><td lang="el">μικρές</td><td lang="el">μικρά</td></tr>
<tr><td>big / large</td><td lang="el">μεγάλοι</td><td lang="el">μεγάλες</td><td lang="el">μεγάλα</td></tr>
<tr><td>good</td><td lang="el">καλοί</td><td lang="el">καλές</td><td lang="el">καλά</td></tr>
<tr><td>new</td><td lang="el">καινούριοι</td><td lang="el">καινούριες</td><td lang="el">καινούρια</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Οι γάτες είναι <strong>μικρές</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The cats are <strong>small</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα σπίτια είναι <strong>μεγάλα</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The houses are <strong>big</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 3. Before a noun

Put the adjective between the article and the noun: **το μικρό σπίτι** (the small house), **μια μεγάλη τσάντα** (a big bag).

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω ένα <strong>καινούριο</strong> βιβλίο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have a <strong>new</strong> book.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Οι <strong>μικροί</strong> σκύλοι πίνουν νερό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The <strong>small</strong> dogs are drinking water.</p>
</div>

## 4. Adjectives with direct objects

Adjectives also agree with a noun in the accusative. For the adjective patterns in this lesson, the masculine form changes. The feminine and neuter forms stay the same.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Gender</th><th scope="col">Nominative</th><th scope="col">Accusative</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>masculine</td><td lang="el">μικρός</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td></tr>
<tr><td>feminine</td><td lang="el">μικρή</td><td lang="el">μικρή</td></tr>
<tr><td>neuter</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td><td lang="el">μικρό</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Gender</th><th scope="col">Nominative</th><th scope="col">Accusative</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>masculine plural</td><td lang="el">μικροί</td><td lang="el">μικρούς</td></tr>
<tr><td>feminine plural</td><td lang="el">μικρές</td><td lang="el">μικρές</td></tr>
<tr><td>neuter plural</td><td lang="el">μικρά</td><td lang="el">μικρά</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω έναν <strong>μικρό</strong> σκύλο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have a <strong>small</strong> dog.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω <strong>μικρούς</strong> σκύλους.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have <strong>small</strong> dogs.</p>
</div>

## 5. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek.

1. The house is small.
2. The dogs are big.
3. My sister is happy.
4. The children are tired.
5. We have a big bag.
6. Maria has two new books.
7. Are the cats small?
8. The apples are good.
9. My brother has a small dog.
10. The bags are not new.
