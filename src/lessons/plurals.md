## 1. Plural subjects

For plural subjects, the definite article (“the”) changes from **ο** or **η** to **οι**, and from **το** to **τα**. The noun ending also changes. These are the nominative forms.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Pattern</th><th scope="col">Singular</th><th scope="col">Plural</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Masculine -ος → -οι</td><td lang="el">ο σκύλος</td><td lang="el"><strong>οι σκύλοι</strong></td><td>the dog → the dogs</td></tr>
<tr><td>Feminine -α → -ες</td><td lang="el">η γάτα</td><td lang="el"><strong>οι γάτες</strong></td><td>the cat → the cats</td></tr>
<tr><td>Feminine -η → -ες</td><td lang="el">η φίλη</td><td lang="el"><strong>οι φίλες</strong></td><td>the friend → the friends (female)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Neuter -ο → -α</td><td lang="el">το βιβλίο</td><td lang="el"><strong>τα βιβλία</strong></td><td>the book → the books</td></tr>
<tr><td>Neuter -ι → -ια</td><td lang="el">το σπίτι</td><td lang="el"><strong>τα σπίτια</strong></td><td>the house → the houses</td></tr>
<tr><td>Neuter -ί → -ιά</td><td lang="el">το παιδί</td><td lang="el"><strong>τα παιδιά</strong></td><td>the child → the children</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

These are common patterns, not rules for every noun. Notice the accent in **βιβλίο → βιβλία** and **παιδί → παιδιά**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Οι γάτες</strong> είναι εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>The cats</strong> are here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Οι σκύλοι</strong> πίνουν νερό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>The dogs</strong> are drinking water.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Τα παιδιά</strong> μένουν εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>The children</strong> live here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Οι φίλες</strong> έχουν έναν σκύλο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>The friends</strong> have a dog. (Female friends.)</p>
</div>

## 2. Plural objects

For plural direct objects, use the accusative articles **τους, τις, τα**. Masculine nouns ending in **-οι** change to **-ους**. Feminine plurals in **-ες** and all neuter plurals keep the same noun form in the accusative.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Gender</th><th scope="col">Nominative</th><th scope="col">Accusative</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Masculine</td><td lang="el">οι σκύλοι</td><td lang="el"><strong>τους σκύλους</strong></td><td>the dogs</td></tr>
<tr><td>Feminine</td><td lang="el">οι τσάντες</td><td lang="el"><strong>τις τσάντες</strong></td><td>the bags</td></tr>
<tr><td>Neuter</td><td lang="el">τα βιβλία</td><td lang="el"><strong>τα βιβλία</strong></td><td>the books</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα παιδιά πίνουν <strong>τους χυμούς</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The children are drinking <strong>the juices</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω <strong>τις τσάντες</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have <strong>the bags</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχουμε <strong>τα βιβλία</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We have <strong>the books</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 3. Without an article

**Ένας, μια, ένα** have no plural forms. When saying that someone has books, cats, or similar things without specifying which ones, use the plural noun without an article:

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω <strong>βιβλία</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have <strong>books</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα σπίτια έχουν <strong>πόρτες</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The houses have <strong>doors</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 4. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek.

1. The dogs are here.
2. The children are drinking juice.
3. The bags are not here.
4. Are the pencils here?
5. We have the cats.
6. Maria has the books.
7. They drink juices.
8. I don't have the bags.
9. Do you have the dogs? (Singular.)
10. The cats live here.
