Compare the tenses using the same activity: watching a film.

## 1. The main patterns

Learn these irregular forms together: **βλέπω → είδα → θα δω**. Ongoing forms use βλεπ-, the simple past uses ειδ-, and the simple future and subjunctive use forms such as δω, δεις, and δει.

**Simple** forms present an action as a whole. **Continuous** forms present it as ongoing or habitual. This is aspect: it does not simply mean “once” versus “many times.”

## 2. The tenses side by side

<tr><td>Continuous future</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα βλέπω</strong> μια ταινία κάθε Παρασκευή.</td><td>I will watch a film every Friday.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Simple future</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα δω</strong> μια ταινία απόψε.</td><td>I will watch a film tonight.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Present perfect</td><td lang="el"><strong>Έχω δει</strong> αυτή την ταινία.</td><td>I have seen this film.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Past perfect</td><td lang="el"><strong>Είχα δει</strong> την ταινία πριν έρθεις.</td><td>I had seen the film before you arrived.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Future perfect</td><td lang="el">Μέχρι αύριο, <strong>θα έχω δει</strong> την ταινία.</td><td>By tomorrow, I will have seen the film.</td></tr>

The present can describe either an action happening now or a habit. The imperfect describes a past activity or habit; the simple past views the event as a whole. Perfect tenses relate an earlier action to the present, a past point, or a future point.

In perfect forms, **δει** stays unchanged; έχω changes for the person and time.

## 3. With να: the subjunctive

After να, the simple, continuous, and perfect forms retain the same aspect distinctions. These are not additional tenses.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Form and use</th><th scope="col">Greek example</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Simple: action as a whole</td><td lang="el">Θέλω <strong>να δω</strong> τη Μαρία.</td><td>I want to see Maria.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Continuous: a habit</td><td lang="el">Θέλω <strong>να βλέπω</strong> τη Μαρία πιο συχνά.</td><td>I want to see Maria more often.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Perfect: completed by a reference time</td><td lang="el">Θέλω <strong>να έχω δει</strong> την ταινία μέχρι αύριο.</td><td>I want to have seen the film by tomorrow.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

For the simple and continuous conjugations, replace θα in the future tables with να. For the perfect, put να before the present-perfect form.

## 4. Commands

Imperatives address one person informally or several people / one person politely. Negative commands use **μη(ν) + subjunctive**, not imperative forms.

## 5. Other active forms

Hypothetical meanings use θα with past forms. The adverbial participle expresses an accompanying action and does not change for person or number.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Construction</th><th scope="col">Greek example</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Hypothetical</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα έβλεπα</strong> αυτή την ταινία, αλλά δεν έχω χρόνο.</td><td>I would watch this film, but I don’t have time.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Hypothetical past</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα είχα δει</strong> την ταινία, αλλά ήμουν άρρωστη.</td><td>I would have watched the film, but I was ill. (Female speaker.)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Adverbial participle</td><td lang="el">Έμαθα ελληνικές λέξεις <strong>βλέποντας</strong> ταινίες.</td><td>I learned Greek words by watching films.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

## 6. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek using **βλέπω**.

1. You see Maria every day. (One person.)

2. We were watching a film when you arrived.

3. We saw Maria yesterday.

4. You will see Maria tomorrow. (One person.)

5. They will see Maria every Friday. (A future habit.)

6. We have seen the film.

7. They had seen the film before you arrived.

8. By tomorrow, we will have seen the film.

9. Look at this photograph! (To several people.)

10. I want to watch films more often.
