## 1. The three genders

Every Greek noun has a grammatical gender: **masculine**, **feminine**, or **neuter**. The word for “the” shows the gender:

- **ο** for masculine nouns.
- **η** for feminine nouns.
- **το** for neuter nouns.

Learn each noun together with its article.

Gender is a feature of the word, not necessarily the sex of the person or animal. For example, **το κορίτσι** (the girl) is grammatically neuter.

## 2. Clues in the endings

Some common singular endings can help you recognise gender:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Gender</th><th scope="col">Common endings</th><th scope="col">Examples</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Masculine</td><td lang="el">-ος</td><td lang="el">ο σκύλ<strong>ος</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>Feminine</td><td lang="el">-α, -η</td><td lang="el">η γάτ<strong>α</strong>, η φίλ<strong>η</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>Neuter</td><td lang="el">-ο, -ι</td><td lang="el">το βιβλί<strong>ο</strong>, το σπίτ<strong>ι</strong></td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

These are clues, not rules for every noun. The article is your guide: learn **το βιβλίο**, rather than βιβλίο alone.

## 3. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek, including the article.

1. The dog.

2. The cat.

3. The book.

4. The male friend.

5. The female friend.

6. The door.

7. The house.

8. The child.

9. The road.

10. The book and the house. (Use **και** for “and.”)
