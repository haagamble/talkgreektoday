## 3. To, in, and at: σε

**Σε** can mean “to,” “in,” or “at.” It takes the accusative and combines with the definite article:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Combination</th><th scope="col">Form</th><th scope="col">Example</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">σε + τον</td><td lang="el">στον</td><td lang="el">στον σταθμό</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">σε + τη(ν)</td><td lang="el">στη(ν)</td><td lang="el">στη δουλειά / στην τράπεζα</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">σε + το</td><td lang="el">στο</td><td lang="el">στο πάρκο</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

**Στην** follows the same final **-ν** rule as **την**. Keep the **-ν** in masculine **στον**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πάω <strong>στο σχολείο</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m going <strong>to school</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είμαι <strong>στο σχολείο</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m <strong>at school</strong>.</p>
</div>

With an indefinite article, keep **σε** separate: **σε ένα σπίτι** (in a house).

## 4. Plural destinations and locations

With plural definite articles, **σε** combines with the article:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Combination</th><th scope="col">Form</th><th scope="col">Example</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">σε + τους</td><td lang="el">στους</td><td lang="el">στους σταθμούς</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">σε + τις</td><td lang="el">στις</td><td lang="el">στις τράπεζες</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">σε + τα</td><td lang="el">στα</td><td lang="el">στα σχολεία</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

**Το σχολείο → τα σχολεία** follows the neuter **-ο → -α** pattern.

## 5. Example sentences

- **πού** — where

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πού πας;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Where are you going? (Singular, informal.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πού είναι η τράπεζα;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Where is the bank?</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δεν πάμε <strong>στο πάρκο</strong> τώρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We’re not going <strong>to the park</strong> now.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Μαρία δεν είναι <strong>στη δουλειά</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Maria is not <strong>at work</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πάω <strong>στο σούπερ μάρκετ</strong> τώρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m going <strong>to the supermarket</strong> now.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πάμε <strong>στο σχολείο</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We’re going <strong>to school</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Μαρία πάει <strong>στην τράπεζα</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Maria is going <strong>to the bank</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είμαι <strong>στο σπίτι</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m <strong>at home</strong>.</p>

<div class="example">

<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Are you going <strong>to the restaurant</strong>? (Plural or polite.)</p>
</div>

## 6. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek.

1. We are going to the supermarket.
2. My father is at the station.
3. The children go to school every day.
4. Are you going to work now? (Singular, informal.)
5. Maria is at the restaurant.
6. Where are you going? (Plural or polite.)
7. My sister is not going to the bank.
8. We are at home.
9. They live in a small house.
10. Where is the school?
