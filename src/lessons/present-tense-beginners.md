## 1. One present-tense form

Greek has no separate present continuous form. **Πίνω** can mean “I drink” or “I am drinking.”

- **το νερό** — water
- **τώρα** — now
- **κάθε μέρα** — every day

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πίνω νερό τώρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I'm drinking water now.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πίνω νερό κάθε μέρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I drink water every day.</p>
</div>

## 2. The six persons: πίνω (to drink)

The verb changes its ending depending on who does the action. Greek often leaves out the subject pronoun because the verb ending tells us who it is.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Person</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>εγώ</td><td lang="el">πίνω</td><td>I drink</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσύ</td><td lang="el">πίνεις</td><td>you drink (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el">πίνει</td><td>he / she / it drinks</td></tr>
<tr><td>εμείς</td><td lang="el">πίνουμε</td><td>we drink</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσείς</td><td lang="el">πίνετε</td><td>you drink (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el">πίνουν(ε)</td><td>they drink</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

Notice the common endings: **-ω, -εις, -ει, -ουμε, -ετε, -ουν(ε)**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πίνουμε νερό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We drink water.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πίνουν νερό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">They drink water.</p>
</div>

## 3. Two more useful verbs

**μένω** (to live / stay) follows the same endings. **έχω** (to have) is similar but has a few different forms.

### μένω (to live / stay)

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Person</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>εγώ</td><td lang="el">μένω</td><td>I live / stay</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσύ</td><td lang="el">μένεις</td><td>you live / stay (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el">μένει</td><td>he / she / it lives / stays</td></tr>
<tr><td>εμείς</td><td lang="el">μένουμε</td><td>we live / stay</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσείς</td><td lang="el">μένετε</td><td>you live / stay (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el">μένουν(ε)</td><td>they live / stay</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

- **η Ελλάδα** — Greece
- **η Αθήνα** — Athens

Learn **στην Ελλάδα** (in Greece) and **στην Αθήνα** (in Athens) as whole expressions for now.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Μένω στην Ελλάδα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I live in Greece.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Μένουμε εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We live here.</p>
</div>

### έχω (to have)

<thead><tr><th scope="col">Person</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>εγώ</td><td lang="el">έχω</td><td>I have</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσύ</td><td lang="el">έχεις</td><td>you have (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el">έχει</td><td>he / she / it has</td></tr>
<tr><td>εμείς</td><td lang="el">έχουμε</td><td>we have</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσείς</td><td lang="el">έχετε</td><td>you have (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el">έχουν(ε)</td><td>they have</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχουμε ένα παιδί.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We have a child.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Μαρία έχει ένα σπίτι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Maria has a house.</p>
</div>

Greek normally uses the definite article before people’s names.

## 4. Questions

To ask a yes/no question, keep the same verb form and use a questioning tone.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Μένεις στην Αθήνα;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Do you live in Athens?</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχεις ένα παιδί;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Do you have a child?</p>
</div>

## 5. Negatives

- **το αυτοκίνητο** — car

To make a negative sentence, put **δεν** before the verb.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δεν μένω στην Αθήνα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I don't live in Athens.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δεν έχω αυτοκίνητο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I don't have a car.</p>
</div>

## 6. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek. Use the present tense.

1. He lives in Athens.

2. You drink water every day. (Singular.)

3. We don't live here.

4. He is drinking water now.

5. I have a book.

6. She has a cat.

7. They have a child.

8. Do you (plural / polite) have a car?

9. Are you drinking water now? (Plural or polite.)

10. I don't drink water.
