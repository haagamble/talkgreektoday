Compare the tenses using the same action: opening a door.

## 1. The main patterns

Learn these forms together: **ανοίγω → άνοιξα → θα ανοίξω**. Ongoing forms use ανοιγ-; completed-action forms use ανοιξ-. Notice the stress change in άνοιξα.

**Simple** forms present an action as a whole. **Continuous** forms present it as ongoing or habitual. This is aspect: it does not simply mean “once” versus “many times.”

## 2. The tenses side by side

<thead><tr><th scope="col">Tense</th><th scope="col">Greek example</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Present</td><td lang="el"><strong>Ανοίγω</strong> την πόρτα.</td><td>I open / am opening the door.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Imperfect</td><td lang="el"><strong>Άνοιγα</strong> την πόρτα κάθε πρωί.</td><td>I used to open the door every morning.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Simple past</td><td lang="el"><strong>Άνοιξα</strong> την πόρτα χθες.</td><td>I opened the door yesterday.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Continuous future</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα ανοίγω</strong> την πόρτα κάθε πρωί.</td><td>I will open the door every morning.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Simple future</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα ανοίξω</strong> την πόρτα αύριο.</td><td>I will open the door tomorrow.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Present perfect</td><td lang="el"><strong>Έχω ανοίξει</strong> την πόρτα.</td><td>I have opened the door.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Past perfect</td><td lang="el"><strong>Είχα ανοίξει</strong> την πόρτα πριν έρθεις.</td><td>I had opened the door before you arrived.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Future perfect</td><td lang="el">Μέχρι τις οκτώ, <strong>θα έχω ανοίξει</strong> την πόρτα.</td><td>By eight, I will have opened the door.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

The present can describe either an action happening now or a habit. The imperfect describes a past activity or habit; the simple past views the event as a whole. Perfect tenses relate an earlier action to the present, a past point, or a future point.

In perfect forms, **ανοίξει** stays unchanged; έχω changes for the person and time.

## 3. With να: the subjunctive

After να, the simple, continuous, and perfect forms retain the same aspect distinctions. These are not additional tenses.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Form and use</th><th scope="col">Greek example</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Simple: action as a whole</td><td lang="el">Θέλω <strong>να ανοίξω</strong> την πόρτα.</td><td>I want to open the door.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Continuous: a habit</td><td lang="el">Θέλω <strong>να ανοίγω</strong> τα παράθυρα κάθε πρωί.</td><td>I want to open the windows every morning.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Perfect: completed by a reference time</td><td lang="el">Θέλω <strong>να έχω ανοίξει</strong> το μαγαζί μέχρι τις οκτώ.</td><td>I want to have opened the shop by eight.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

For the simple and continuous conjugations, replace θα in the future tables with να. For the perfect, put να before the present-perfect form.

## 4. Commands

Imperatives address one person informally or several people / one person politely. Negative commands use **μη(ν) + subjunctive**, not imperative forms.

## 5. Other active forms

Hypothetical meanings use θα with past forms. The adverbial participle expresses an accompanying action and does not change for person or number.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Construction</th><th scope="col">Greek example</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Hypothetical</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα άνοιγα</strong> την πόρτα, αλλά δεν έχω το κλειδί.</td><td>I would open the door, but I don’t have the key.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Hypothetical past</td><td lang="el"><strong>Θα είχα ανοίξει</strong> την πόρτα, αλλά δεν είχα το κλειδί.</td><td>I would have opened the door, but I didn’t have the key.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Adverbial participle</td><td lang="el"><strong>Ανοίγοντας</strong> την πόρτα, είδα τη γάτα.</td><td>Opening the door, I saw the cat.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

## 6. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek using **ανοίγω**.

1. We open the door every morning.

2. You used to open the windows every morning. (One person.)

3. They opened the door yesterday.

4. I will open the window tomorrow.

5. We will open the windows every morning. (A future habit.)

6. We have opened the door.

7. She had opened the shop before you arrived.

8. By eight, we will have opened the shop.

9. Open the door! (To several people.)

10. I want to open the window.
