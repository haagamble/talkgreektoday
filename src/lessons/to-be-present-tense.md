## 2. The verb “to be”

Modern Greek does not use an infinitive like English “to be.” Instead, dictionaries list verbs in the “I” form: **είμαι** means “I am.”

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Subject pronoun</th><th scope="col">Verb</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">εγώ</td><td lang="el"><strong>είμαι</strong></td><td>I am</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εσύ</td><td lang="el"><strong>είσαι</strong></td><td>you are (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el"><strong>είναι</strong></td><td>he / she / it is</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εμείς</td><td lang="el"><strong>είμαστε</strong></td><td>we are</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εσείς</td><td lang="el"><strong>είστε</strong></td><td>you are (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el"><strong>είναι</strong></td><td>they are</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

Use **αυτοί** for a group of men or a mixed-gender group, **αυτές** for a group of women, and **αυτά** for a neuter group or things.

The pronoun can be left out: **Είμαι** already means “I am.” Use the pronoun when you want to make the subject clear or emphasise it. **Είναι** can mean “he/she/it is” or “they are,” depending on the context.

## 3. Four adjectives

The adjective’s form matches the gender of the person or noun it describes.

These are singular forms. We’ll learn the plural adjective forms later.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Masculine singular</th><th scope="col">Feminine singular</th><th scope="col">Neuter singular</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>ready</td><td lang="el">έτοιμος</td><td lang="el">έτοιμη</td><td lang="el">έτοιμο</td></tr>
<tr><td>tired</td><td lang="el">κουρασμένος</td><td lang="el">κουρασμένη</td><td lang="el">κουρασμένο</td></tr>
<tr><td>happy</td><td lang="el">χαρούμενος</td><td lang="el">χαρούμενη</td><td lang="el">χαρούμενο</td></tr>
<tr><td>sad</td><td lang="el">λυπημένος</td><td lang="el">λυπημένη</td><td lang="el">λυπημένο</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είμαι <strong>κουρασμένη</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m tired. (A woman speaking.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Αυτός είναι <strong>έτοιμος</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">He is ready.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το παιδί είναι <strong>χαρούμενο</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The child is happy.</p>
</div>

Use the neuter form with **το παιδί**, whether the child is a boy or a girl.

## 4. With εδώ and εκεί

- **εδώ** — here
- **εκεί** — there

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Είμαστε</strong> εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We are here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Αυτοί <strong>είναι</strong> εκεί.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">They are there. (A masculine or mixed-gender group.)</p>
</div>



## 5. Questions

For a yes/no question, keep the same word order and use a questioning tone. The Greek question mark is **;**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Είσαι</strong> έτοιμος;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Are you ready? (To one man, informally.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Είστε</strong> εκεί;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Are you there? (To several people, or one person politely.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Είναι</strong> χαρούμενη;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Is she happy?</p>
</div>

## 6. Negatives

Put **δεν** before the verb to say “not.”

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Δεν είμαι</strong> λυπημένη.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m not sad. (A woman speaking.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το παιδί <strong>δεν είναι</strong> εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The child isn’t here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Αυτός <strong>δεν είναι</strong> κουρασμένος.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">He is not tired.</p>
</div>

## 7. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek. Use the gender or number indicated.

1. I am here.

2. You are ready. (To one woman, informally.)

3. The dog is tired.

4. The child is sad.

5. The child is not there.

6. Are you here? (To several people.)

7. They are not here. (A masculine group.)

8. The house is not ready.

9. Is she tired?

10. She is happy.
