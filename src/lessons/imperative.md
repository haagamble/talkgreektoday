The imperative (η προστακτική) expresses commands, requests, instructions, and advice.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Κλείσε την πόρτα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Close the door.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Περίμενε!</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Wait!</p>
</div>

## 1. Singular and plural commands

Use the singular when addressing one person informally. Use the plural for several people or when addressing one person politely.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Singular</th><th scope="col">Plural / polite</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Close!</td><td lang="el">Κλείσε!</td><td lang="el">Κλείστε!</td></tr>
<tr><td>Open!</td><td lang="el">Άνοιξε!</td><td lang="el">Ανοίξτε!</td></tr>
<tr><td>Wait!</td><td lang="el">Περίμενε!</td><td lang="el">Περιμένετε!</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

The imperative verb does not change according to the gender of the person addressed.

## 2. Simple and continuous commands

The **simple imperative** (συνοπτική προστακτική) presents an action as a whole. The **continuous imperative** (εξακολουθητική προστακτική) presents it as ongoing or repeated.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col"></th><th scope="col">Simple</th><th scope="col">Continuous</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">Singular</th><td lang="el">γράψε</td><td lang="el">γράφε</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Plural / polite</th><td lang="el">γράψτε</td><td lang="el">γράφετε</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Γράψε</strong> το όνομά σου.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Write your name. (One complete task.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Γράφε</strong> κάθε μέρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Write every day. (A repeated action.)</p>
</div>

The distinction concerns how the action is viewed, not its length: a long task can still take the simple imperative when presented as a whole.

## 3. Forming the simple imperative

For many active verbs, use the completed-action stem with **-ε** for the singular and **-τε** for the plural. This is the stem also used in the simple future: θα γράψω → γραψ- → γράψε / γράψτε.

The stem may differ from the present tense, and the stress may move. Learn the forms together rather than adding endings directly to the present-tense verb.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Present tense</th><th scope="col">Singular</th><th scope="col">Plural / polite</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">κλείνω — close</td><td lang="el">κλείσε</td><td lang="el">κλείστε</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">γράφω — write</td><td lang="el">γράψε</td><td lang="el">γράψτε</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ανοίγω — open</td><td lang="el">άνοιξε</td><td lang="el">ανοίξτε</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">στέλνω — send</td><td lang="el">στείλε</td><td lang="el">στείλτε</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

Verbs ending in -άω / -ώ often use a stem ending in -ησ-:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Present tense</th><th scope="col">Simple singular</th><th scope="col">Simple plural / polite</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">μιλάω — speak</td><td lang="el">μίλησε</td><td lang="el">μιλήστε</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ρωτάω — ask</td><td lang="el">ρώτησε</td><td lang="el">ρωτήστε</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">βοηθάω — help</td><td lang="el">βοήθησε</td><td lang="el">βοηθήστε</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

You will also hear μίλα and ρώτα. Grammatically, these are continuous forms, with plurals μιλάτε and ρωτάτε; in everyday requests they can also refer to a single occasion. Keep them distinct from the simple pairs above.

## 5. Negative commands

Use **μη(ν) + subjunctive**, not δεν + imperative. The subjunctive here appears without να. Use its simple form for an action viewed as a whole and its continuous form for an ongoing or repeated action.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Positive</th><th scope="col">Negative</th><th scope="col">Meaning of the negative</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">Άνοιξε την πόρτα.</td><td lang="el">Μην ανοίξεις την πόρτα.</td><td>Don’t open the door.</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">Γράψε το όνομά σου.</td><td lang="el">Μη γράψεις το όνομά σου.</td><td>Don’t write your name.</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">Γράφε κάθε μέρα.</td><td lang="el">Μη γράφεις κάθε μέρα.</td><td>Don’t write every day.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

Notice the endings: γράψε becomes μη γράψεις, while γράφε becomes μη γράφεις.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Μην ανησυχείς!</strong></p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Don’t worry!</p>
</div>

## 6. Object pronouns with commands

- **με** — me
- **το** — it
- **μου** — to me

With positive imperatives, unstressed object pronouns follow the verb. With negative commands, they come before it.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Positive</th><th scope="col">Negative</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">Βοήθησέ με!</td><td lang="el">Μη με βοηθήσεις!</td><td>Help me! / Don’t help me!</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">Πάρε το!</td><td lang="el">Μην το πάρεις!</td><td>Take it! / Don’t take it!</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">Δώσε μου το!</td><td lang="el">Μη μου το δώσεις!</td><td>Give it to me! / Don’t give it to me!</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

### An extra accent

When the verb is stressed on its third-to-last syllable, an unstressed pronoun after it adds a second accent on the verb’s last syllable: **βοήθησε → βοήθησέ με**.

## 7. Polite requests

- **παρακαλώ** — please
- **πιο αργά** — more slowly

Use the plural form when addressing someone politely. Add **παρακαλώ** to a request.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Μιλήστε</strong> πιο αργά, <strong>παρακαλώ</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Please speak more slowly.</p>
</div>

## 8. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek. Address one person informally unless otherwise indicated.

1. Close the door. (To several people.)

2. Come in!

3. Write every day. (To several people; a repeated action.)

4. Don’t close the door. (One particular action.)

5. Don’t write every day. (To several people; a repeated action.)

6. Help me, please!

7. Give me some water.

8. Don’t give me water. (One particular action.)

9. Please speak more slowly.

10. Drink some water. (To several people.)
