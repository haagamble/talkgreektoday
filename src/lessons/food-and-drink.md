## 1. Food

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">το ψωμί</td><td>bread</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το τυρί</td><td>cheese</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το αυγό</td><td>egg</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το κοτόπουλο</td><td>chicken</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το ρύζι</td><td>rice</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η σαλάτα</td><td>salad</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η σοκολάτα</td><td>chocolate</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

When talking about an unspecified amount of food or drink, leave out the article: **ψωμί** (bread), **τυρί** (cheese), **νερό** (water).

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχουμε <strong>ψωμί και τυρί</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We have <strong>bread and cheese</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Μαρία έχει <strong>δύο αυγά</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Maria has <strong>two eggs</strong>.</p>
</div>

**Το αυγό** becomes **τα αυγά** in the plural.

## 2. Fruit and vegetables

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">το μήλο</td><td>apple</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η μπανάνα</td><td>banana</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το πορτοκάλι</td><td>orange</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η ντομάτα</td><td>tomato</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η πατάτα</td><td>potato</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

The plural forms in this group are **μήλα, μπανάνες, πορτοκάλια, ντομάτες, πατάτες**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω <strong>τρία μήλα</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have <strong>three apples</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχουμε <strong>μπανάνες και πορτοκάλια</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We have <strong>bananas and oranges</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 3. Drinks

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">ο καφές</td><td>coffee</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το γάλα</td><td>milk</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το τσάι</td><td>tea</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το νερό</td><td>water</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο χυμός</td><td>juice</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

For a direct object, **ο καφές** becomes **τον καφέ**. Use **έναν καφέ** for “a coffee.”

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πίνω <strong>καφέ</strong> κάθε μέρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I drink <strong>coffee</strong> every day.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα παιδιά πίνουν <strong>γάλα</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The children are drinking <strong>milk</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 4. Eating: τρώω

**Τρώω** means “I eat” or “I am eating.” Its present-tense forms have a different pattern from πίνω:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Person</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">εγώ</td><td lang="el">τρώω</td><td>I eat</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εσύ</td><td lang="el">τρως</td><td>you eat (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el">τρώει</td><td>he / she / it eats</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εμείς</td><td lang="el">τρώμε</td><td>we eat</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εσείς</td><td lang="el">τρώτε</td><td>you eat (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el">τρώνε</td><td>they eat</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Τρώω</strong> κοτόπουλο και ρύζι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m <strong>eating</strong> chicken and rice.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Μαρία <strong>τρώει</strong> μια σαλάτα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Maria is <strong>eating</strong> a salad.</p>
</div>

## 5. Saying what you want: θέλω

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Person</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">εγώ</td><td lang="el">θέλω</td><td>I want</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εσύ</td><td lang="el">θέλεις</td><td>you want (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el">θέλει</td><td>he / she / it wants</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εμείς</td><td lang="el">θέλουμε</td><td>we want</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">εσείς</td><td lang="el">θέλετε</td><td>you want (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el">θέλουν(ε)</td><td>they want</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Θέλεις</strong> έναν χυμό;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Do you <strong>want</strong> a juice? (Singular.)</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Δεν θέλω</strong> σοκολάτα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I <strong>don’t want</strong> chocolate.</p>
</div>

## 6. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek.

1. We are eating bread and cheese.
2. Do you want a coffee? (Singular.)
3. Maria has four tomatoes.
4. I am not drinking milk now.
5. The children are eating bananas.
6. We want chicken and potatoes.
7. Do you eat eggs every day? (Plural or polite.)
8. She wants an apple and an orange.
9. They have rice and salad.
10. I want tea and chocolate.
