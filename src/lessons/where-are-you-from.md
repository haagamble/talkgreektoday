## 3. From: από

- **από** — from

Use the accusative after **από**:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Country</th><th scope="col">From…</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">η Ελλάδα</td><td lang="el">από την Ελλάδα</td><td>from Greece</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Γαλλία</td><td lang="el">από τη Γαλλία</td><td>from France</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Κύπρος</td><td lang="el">από την Κύπρο</td><td>from Cyprus</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το Ηνωμένο Βασίλειο</td><td lang="el">από το Ηνωμένο Βασίλειο</td><td>from the United Kingdom</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο Καναδάς</td><td lang="el">από τον Καναδά</td><td>from Canada</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

**Κύπρος** is feminine despite ending in **-ος**. Its accusative is **Κύπρο**. Masculine **Καναδάς** becomes **Καναδά**. The other feminine country names in this lesson keep the same noun form.

The final **-ν** in **την** follows the sound rule from the accusative lesson. Notice **από την Ελλάδα**, but **από τη Γαλλία**. Keep **τον** with masculine names.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είμαι <strong>από την Ελλάδα</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m <strong>from Greece</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η μητέρα μου είναι <strong>από τον Καναδά</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">My mother is <strong>from Canada</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είμαι <strong>από τη Θεσσαλονίκη</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m <strong>from Thessaloniki</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είναι <strong>από το Λονδίνο</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">He/She is <strong>from London</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 4. Asking where someone is from

- **πού** — where

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">Από πού είσαι;</td><td>Where are you from? (Singular, informal.)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">Από πού είστε;</td><td>Where are you from? (Plural or polite.)</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">Από πού είναι ο πατέρας σου;</td><td>Where is your father from?</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

**Από πού είσαι;** is the most natural way to ask “Where are you from?” The words **από πού** stay together. You can add the subject pronoun for emphasis: **Εσύ από πού είσαι;**

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είμαι από τη Γαλλία. <strong>Εσύ;</strong></p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m from France. <strong>And you?</strong></p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Δεν είμαι</strong> από την Ιταλία.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I’m <strong>not</strong> from Italy.</p>
</div>

## 5. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek.

1. I am from the United States.
2. Maria is from Cyprus.
3. My father is from Germany.
4. Are you from England? (Singular, informal.)
5. We are from Spain.
6. My sister is not from Canada.
7. Where is your mother from?
8. They are from Italy.
9. I am from the United Kingdom.
10. Are you from Greece? (Plural or polite.)
