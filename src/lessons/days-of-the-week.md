## 1. The seven days of the week

Modern calendars in Greece normally show Monday (Δευτέρα) as the first day of the week.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">Pronunciation (approx.)</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Monday</td><td lang="el">η Δευτέρα</td><td>def-TEH-ra</td></tr>
<tr><td>Tuesday</td><td lang="el">η Τρίτη</td><td>TREE-tee</td></tr>
<tr><td>Wednesday</td><td lang="el">η Τετάρτη</td><td>teh-TAR-tee</td></tr>
<tr><td>Thursday</td><td lang="el">η Πέμπτη</td><td>PEM-ptee</td></tr>
<tr><td>Friday</td><td lang="el">η Παρασκευή</td><td>pa-ra-skeh-VEE</td></tr>
<tr><td>Saturday</td><td lang="el">το Σάββατο</td><td>SA-va-to</td></tr>
<tr><td>Sunday</td><td lang="el">η Κυριακή</td><td>kee-ree-a-KEE</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

### A useful pattern

Six of the seven days are feminine and use η.

Saturday is the exception: το Σάββατο is neuter.

In Greek, days of the week are normally capitalized.

The names Δευτέρα, Τρίτη, Τετάρτη, and Πέμπτη are related to the words for second, third, fourth, and fifth. They reflect the traditional numbering of the week, with Sunday as the first day. Παρασκευή also means preparation, while Κυριακή is associated with the Lord's day.

## 2. Saying "on Monday", "on Tuesday", etc.

To say “on” a particular day, use τη(ν) before feminine day names and το before Σάββατο. No preposition is needed.

## 3. Saying "every Monday"

There are two very common ways to express repeated days.

Method A: κάθε + day

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Κάθε Δευτέρα</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Every Monday.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Κάθε Σάββατο</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Every Saturday.</p>
</div>

Method B: τις + plural day (or τα for Saturday)

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τις Δευτέρες</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">On Mondays.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Τα Σάββατα</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">On Saturdays.</p>
</div>

Both constructions are natural and common.

<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">With κάθε</th><th scope="col">With plural article</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Every Monday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Δευτέρα</td><td lang="el">τις Δευτέρες</td></tr>
<tr><td>Every Tuesday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Τρίτη</td><td lang="el">τις Τρίτες</td></tr>
<tr><td>Every Wednesday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Τετάρτη</td><td lang="el">τις Τετάρτες</td></tr>
<tr><td>Every Thursday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Πέμπτη</td><td lang="el">τις Πέμπτες</td></tr>
<tr><td>Every Friday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Παρασκευή</td><td lang="el">τις Παρασκευές</td></tr>
<tr><td>Every Saturday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Σάββατο</td><td lang="el">τα Σάββατα</td></tr>
<tr><td>Every Sunday</td><td lang="el">κάθε Κυριακή</td><td lang="el">τις Κυριακές</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

### Useful sentences

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πηγαίνω στο σούπερ μάρκετ <strong>κάθε Σάββατο</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I go to the supermarket every Saturday.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Πηγαίνω στο σούπερ μάρκετ <strong>τα Σάββατα</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I go to the supermarket on Saturdays.</p>
</div>

These two sentences mean essentially the same thing.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Τις Δευτέρες</strong> δουλεύω από το σπίτι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">On Mondays I work from home.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Κάθε Τετάρτη</strong> κάνω μάθημα ελληνικών.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Every Wednesday I have a Greek lesson.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Τις Κυριακές</strong> συνήθως ξεκουραζόμαστε.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">On Sundays we usually relax.</p>
</div>

## 4. The grammatical forms of the days

Greek nouns change according to their grammatical case. The days of the week are no exception.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Nominative</th><th scope="col">Accusative</th><th scope="col">Genitive</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">η Δευτέρα</td><td lang="el">τη Δευτέρα</td><td lang="el">της Δευτέρας</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Τρίτη</td><td lang="el">την Τρίτη</td><td lang="el">της Τρίτης</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Τετάρτη</td><td lang="el">την Τετάρτη</td><td lang="el">της Τετάρτης</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Πέμπτη</td><td lang="el">την Πέμπτη</td><td lang="el">της Πέμπτης</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Παρασκευή</td><td lang="el">την Παρασκευή</td><td lang="el">της Παρασκευής</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">το Σάββατο</td><td lang="el">το Σάββατο</td><td lang="el">του Σαββάτου</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η Κυριακή</td><td lang="el">την Κυριακή</td><td lang="el">της Κυριακής</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

The nominative is used when the day is the subject of the sentence.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Δευτέρα είναι δύσκολη μέρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Monday is a difficult day.</p>
</div>

The accusative is used in common time expressions.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Θα φύγω την Παρασκευή.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I&#x27;ll leave on Friday.</p>
</div>

The genitive can express a relationship or association.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το πρόγραμμα της Δευτέρας είναι γεμάτο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Monday&#x27;s schedule is full.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το μεσημέρι του Σαββάτου θα είμαστε σπίτι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">On Saturday afternoon we&#x27;ll be at home.</p>
</div>

## 5. Referring to specific days

These expressions use the accusative to say when something happens.

Use these expressions to specify this, next, or last Monday. The same patterns work with the other days; Saturday uses the neuter forms shown below.

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">English</th><th scope="col">Greek</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>this Monday</td><td lang="el">αυτή τη Δευτέρα</td></tr>
<tr><td>next Monday</td><td lang="el">την επόμενη Δευτέρα</td></tr>
<tr><td>last Monday</td><td lang="el">την περασμένη Δευτέρα</td></tr>
<tr><td>this Saturday</td><td lang="el">αυτό το Σάββατο</td></tr>
<tr><td>next Saturday</td><td lang="el">το επόμενο Σάββατο</td></tr>
<tr><td>last Saturday</td><td lang="el">το περασμένο Σάββατο</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

### Examples

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Αυτή τη Δευτέρα έχω ραντεβού.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have an appointment this Monday.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Θα έρθω την επόμενη Τρίτη.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I&#x27;ll come next Tuesday.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είδα τη Μαρία την περασμένη Παρασκευή.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I saw Maria last Friday.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Θα πάμε για φαγητό το επόμενο Σάββατο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We&#x27;ll go out for a meal next Saturday.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Την περασμένη Κυριακή πήγαμε στην παραλία.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Last Sunday we went to the beach.</p>
</div>

A useful colloquial expression: από Δευτέρα

You will frequently hear Greeks say:

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Από Δευτέρα!</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Literally: From Monday!</p>
</div>

It means starting Monday and is often used when talking about a new routine, work, exercise, or plans.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Από Δευτέρα αρχίζω γυμναστήριο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">From Monday, I&#x27;m starting at the gym.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Από Δευτέρα θα διαβάζω ελληνικά κάθε μέρα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Starting Monday, I&#x27;ll study Greek every day.</p>
</div>

The article is commonly omitted in this expression.

These expressions are extremely useful alongside the days of the week.

## 9. Talking about weekdays and working days

- **οι καθημερινές** — weekdays (Monday to Friday, in everyday usage)
- **οι εργάσιμες ημέρες** — working / business days

The distinction matters because a working day may depend on the business or service.

### Examples

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δουλεύω μόνο τις καθημερινές.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I only work on weekdays.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το κατάστημα είναι ανοιχτό τις καθημερινές.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The shop is open on weekdays.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η παράδοση θα γίνει σε τρεις εργάσιμες ημέρες.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Delivery will take place within three working days.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Το γραφείο είναι κλειστό τα Σαββατοκύριακα.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">The office is closed on weekends.</p>
</div>
