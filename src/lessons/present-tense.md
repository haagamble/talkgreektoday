## 1. Greek verbs

Greek verbs are listed in the first-person singular present form—the form used for “I.” This is the form you will see in dictionaries.

This lesson covers:

- **βλέπω** — I see
- **έχω** — I have

These two verbs use the same present-tense endings. This is a common pattern, but other Greek verbs use different patterns.

| Person | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| εγώ | **-ω** |
| εσύ | **-εις** |
| αυτός / αυτή / αυτό | **-ει** |
| εμείς | **-ουμε** |
| εσείς | **-ετε** |
| αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά | **-ουν(ε)** |

## 2. To see: βλέπω

The verb changes its ending depending on who does the action. Greek often leaves out the subject pronoun because the verb ending tells us who it is.

<thead><tr><th scope="col">Person</th><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>εγώ</td><td lang="el">βλέπω</td><td>I see</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσύ</td><td lang="el">βλέπεις</td><td>you see (singular)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτός / αυτή / αυτό</td><td lang="el">βλέπει</td><td>he / she / it sees</td></tr>
<tr><td>εμείς</td><td lang="el">βλέπουμε</td><td>we see</td></tr>
<tr><td>εσείς</td><td lang="el">βλέπετε</td><td>you see (plural / polite)</td></tr>
<tr><td>αυτοί / αυτές / αυτά</td><td lang="el">βλέπουν(ε)</td><td>they see</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

In Greek, some nouns change when they are the object of a verb. We’ll learn those changes later. At this stage, we’re using neuter nouns because their forms don’t change.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Βλέπουμε το βιβλίο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We see the book.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Βλέπουν το σπίτι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">They see the house.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Βλέπει ένα μολύβι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">He/She sees a pencil.</p>
</div>

## 3. To have: έχω

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχουμε ένα παιδί.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We have a child.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Η Μαρία έχει ένα σπίτι.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Maria has a house.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχετε το τετράδιο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">You have the notebook. (Plural or polite.)</p>
</div>

Greek normally uses the definite article before people’s names.

## 4. Questions

To ask a yes/no question, keep the same verb form and use a questioning tone.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Βλέπεις το στυλό;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Do you see the pen?</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Βλέπει το κλειδί;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Does he/she see the key?</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχετε ένα κινητό;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Do you have a cell phone? (Plural or polite.)</p>
</div>

## 5. Negatives

To make a negative sentence, put **δεν** before the verb.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δεν βλέπω το βιβλίο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I don't see the book.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δεν έχουμε το κλειδί.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">We don’t have the key.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Δεν έχουν το κινητό.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">They don’t have the cell phone.</p>
</div>

## 6. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek. Use the present tense.

1. She sees a notebook.

2. You have the cell phone. (To one person, informally.)

3. We don't have a house.

4. He has a pen.

5. I have a child.

6. She has the key.

7. They see a pencil.

8. Do you (plural / polite) have a child?

9. Do you see the notebook? (Plural or polite.)

10. I don't see the book.
