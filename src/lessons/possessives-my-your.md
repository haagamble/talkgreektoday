## 1. Family words

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Singular</th><th scope="col">Plural</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">η μητέρα</td><td lang="el">οι μητέρες</td><td>mother → mothers</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο πατέρας</td><td lang="el">οι πατέρες</td><td>father → fathers</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο αδελφός</td><td lang="el">οι αδελφοί</td><td>brother → brothers</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η αδελφή</td><td lang="el">οι αδελφές</td><td>sister → sisters</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο γιος</td><td lang="el">οι γιοι</td><td>son → sons</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η κόρη</td><td lang="el">οι κόρες</td><td>daughter → daughters</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

You may also see **αδερφός** and **αδερφή** for brother and sister.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Ο πατέρας</strong> είναι εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>The father</strong> is here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Η μητέρα</strong> πίνει καφέ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>The mother</strong> is drinking coffee.</p>
</div>

## 2. My and your

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el"><strong>μου</strong></td><td>my</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el"><strong>σου</strong></td><td>your (singular, informal)</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

Put **μου** or **σου** after the noun. Keep the definite article before it:

<div class="table-scroll"><table>
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Greek</th><th scope="col">English</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td lang="el">η μητέρα <strong>μου</strong></td><td><strong>my</strong> mother</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο αδελφός <strong>μου</strong></td><td><strong>my</strong> brother</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">η μητέρα <strong>σου</strong></td><td><strong>your</strong> mother</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="el">ο αδελφός <strong>σου</strong></td><td><strong>your</strong> brother</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

**Μου** and **σου** stay the same with masculine, feminine, and neuter nouns, and with plurals: **ο γιος μου**, **η κόρη μου**, **το παιδί μου**, **τα παιδιά μου**.

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Ο αδελφός μου</strong> μένει εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>My brother</strong> lives here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Είναι εδώ <strong>η μητέρα σου</strong>;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Is <strong>your mother</strong> here?</p>
</div>

Greek word order is flexible. You can also say **Η μητέρα σου είναι εδώ;** Both orders mean “Is your mother here?” Use a questioning tone.

## 3. Using possessives in sentences

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Ο γιος μου</strong> έχει έναν σκύλο.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>My son</strong> has a dog.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el"><strong>Η κόρη σου</strong> δεν είναι εδώ.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en"><strong>Your daughter</strong> is not here.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχω <strong>το βιβλίο μου</strong>.</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">I have <strong>my book</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="example">
<p class="greek-line" lang="el">Έχεις <strong>τον γιο σου</strong>;</p>
<p class="translation-line" lang="en">Do you have <strong>your son</strong>?</p>
</div>

## 4. Practice exercises

Translate into Greek.

1. My mother is here.
2. Your father is drinking water.
3. I am eating my apple.
4. Is your brother here?
5. Your mother has two daughters.
6. Your sisters live here.
7. My father wants a coffee.
8. You have your books.
9. Your three sons are here.
10. Your children are drinking milk.
